Managerial Insights & Discussion Questions

1. Catalog Volume Drives Spending

Customers who received more catalogs spent significantly more. This is the single most actionable lever — but managers must weigh catalog printing/mailing costs against incremental revenue. Discussion: At what point do marginal catalog costs exceed marginal revenue?

2. Purchase History Predicts Future Value

Customers with High purchase history spend far more than Low or New customers. This suggests a strong case for customer segmentation — target high-history customers with premium catalogs and new customers with introductory offers.

3. The "New Customer" Opportunity

30.3% of customers have no prior purchase history. These are first-time buyers. Their conversion into repeat customers is critical. What onboarding strategies would you recommend?

4. Salary and Spending Relationship

The scatter plot shows a positive correlation between salary and spending. Higher-income customers are more valuable. Consider whether the company should focus acquisition on higher-income demographics.

5. Children Reduce Spending

Customers with more children tend to spend less. Household budget competition from child-related expenses likely reduces discretionary catalog spending. Should the product mix be adjusted for family customers?

6. Location Matters

Compare spending by location (Close vs. Far). Does proximity to a physical store affect catalog purchasing behavior? This has implications for where to target direct mail campaigns geographically.

7. Age Group Strategy

Middle-aged and older customers tend to have higher salaries and spending. The young segment earns less but may have long-term value. How should marketing budgets be allocated across age segments?

8. Homeownership as a Proxy

Homeowners tend to spend differently than renters. Homeownership may serve as a useful proxy variable for financial stability in targeting models. What other demographic proxies could improve targeting?
