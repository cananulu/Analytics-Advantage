Managerial Insights & MBA Discussion Points

1. Promotion 1 Is the Clear Winner

Promotion 1 generates $58.1K avg sales — a 22.8% lift over the weakest promotion (Promo 2 at $47.33K). This is the strongest signal for resource allocation. Is this lift statistically significant? How would you test this?

2. Promotion 2 Consistently Underperforms

Across all market sizes and all weeks, Promotion 2 produces the lowest average sales. Managers should consider discontinuing or redesigning this campaign. What factors could explain why it underperforms?

3. Market Size Drives Baseline Sales

Large markets average $70.12K vs. Medium at $43.99K. Interestingly, Small markets ($57.41K) outperform Medium. Is "market size" capturing something beyond just population? What confounders might exist?

4. Promotion Impact Varies by Market Size

The Promotion x Market Size chart reveals interaction effects. The relative ranking of promotions is consistent, but the absolute gap varies. Should you run different promotions in different market sizes?

5. Sales Are Stable Across Weeks

Average sales barely change from Week 1 to Week 4 (~$53-54K). This suggests no significant time trend or novelty effect — promotions maintain their impact over the study period.

6. Store Age Shows Interesting Patterns

Examine how store age relates to sales. Newer stores and very mature stores may behave differently. Should promotion strategy vary by store maturity?

7. High Location-Level Variance

The Top 10 vs. Bottom 10 locations chart shows a wide gap in performance. Individual store factors (management, local competition, demographics) clearly matter beyond just which promotion is run.

8. Experimental Design Discussion

This dataset is structured as a field experiment. Each location was assigned one promotion and measured over 4 weeks. What are the strengths and limitations of this design? How would you improve it? What about randomization and control groups?
